# Fund glossary (synthetic teaching corpus)

## NAV (net asset value)
NAV is the price of one unit of a mutual fund. It is the
value of everything the fund owns, minus what it owes,
divided by the number of units. NAV is published once a
day, after the market closes.

## Expense ratio
The expense ratio is the yearly fee a fund charges, as a
percentage of your investment. A 1% expense ratio on
Rs 1,00,000 costs about Rs 1,000 a year.

## SIP (systematic investment plan)
A SIP invests a fixed amount every month, like Rs 5,000
on the 5th, through an auto-debit or UPI mandate.

## Exit load
An exit load is a fee charged when you redeem units before a set period, for example
1% if you sell within one year. Liquid funds often have a small exit load for the first
seven days.

## AUM (assets under management)
AUM is the total money a fund manages, usually quoted in crore rupees. A large AUM is not
a sign of good returns by itself.

## Units
When you invest, you buy units at the day's NAV. Investing Rs 10,000 at a NAV of 50 gives
you 200 units.

## Direct and regular plans
A direct plan is bought straight from the fund house, with no distributor commission, so
its expense ratio is lower than the regular plan of the same fund.

## Lump sum
A lump sum is a single one-time investment of a large amount.

## SWP (systematic withdrawal plan)
An SWP redeems a fixed amount every month, often used for regular income in retirement.

## ELSS (equity linked savings scheme)
ELSS funds are tax-saving equity funds with a three-year lock-in period. You cannot
redeem units before the lock-in ends.

## Volatility
Volatility measures how much a fund's daily returns swing. It is usually the annualised
standard deviation of daily returns. Small cap funds are more volatile than debt funds.

## Momentum
Momentum is the recent return of a fund, for example over three months. Funds with strong
momentum sometimes keep rising for a while, but momentum can reverse.

## Benchmark
A benchmark is the index a fund is compared against, such as a large cap index for a
large cap fund.
