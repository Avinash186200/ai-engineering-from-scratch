# Behavioural answers: the STAR shape

Most behavioural questions are the same five or six stories told from different angles. Write them once,
rehearse them out loud, and keep each under two minutes.

## The shape

| Part | Length | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Situation | 1 sentence | Who, what, and why it mattered |
| Task | 1 sentence | What you personally owned |
| Action | 4-5 sentences | Your decisions, the trade-offs, what you did (say "I", not "we", for your part) |
| Result | 2 sentences | The outcome with a number or a reaction, then the lesson you kept |

## Story bank (fill in your own)

| # | Story type | Questions it answers |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | A system you built end to end | "Walk me through a project", "What are you proudest of?" |
| 2 | Telling an uncomfortable truth (results weaker than hoped, a deadline that would slip) | integrity, stakeholder communication |
| 3 | A mistake or outage you caused, and the fix | ownership, "tell me about a failure" |
| 4 | Disagreement in a code review or design discussion | conflict, collaboration, receiving feedback |
| 5 | Leading or growing people | leadership, mentoring, hiring |
| 6 | Learning something new fast | adaptability, "how do you keep up?" |

## Worked example (generic)

> **Situation**: Our team shipped a churn-prediction model whose backtest looked excellent, and a stakeholder wanted
> to announce it the same week.
> **Task**: I owned the validation before release.
> **Action**: I re-ran the evaluation with a strict time-based split and found that one feature was computed using
> data from after the prediction date. I removed it, re-trained, and the lift fell by about half. I wrote a one-page
> note showing both numbers and why the second was the honest one, and I proposed a two-week pilot instead of a launch.
> **Result**: The pilot matched the corrected number within a few percent, so the model launched with expectations
> people could trust. Since then every model I review gets a leakage check before anyone sees a headline number.

## Questions to have ready

- Tell me about yourself (90 seconds: present role, two achievements, why this job).
- Why are you leaving? (30 seconds, forward-looking, no complaints about anyone.)
- Why this role? (Connect your past strengths to what the team needs.)
- What do you not know yet? (Name it, and say how you would close the gap.)
- Questions for them: always have three, about the team, how success is measured, and the hardest current problem.

## Delivery tips

- Lead with the headline, then the detail. Stop when the story is done.
- Use one number per story.
- Do not blame anyone, even when someone else was at fault.
